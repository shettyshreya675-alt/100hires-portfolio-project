Everybody is using the same playbook. That's the problem.

Do this instead…

Sit down with your team and reverse engineer all of your closed won deals in the last year..

Go back in time:

-3-6 months for SMB/Mid-Market
-12-18 months for Enterprise

What were the signs, signals, catalysts that got your customers to actually buy?

You can even get Claude to analyze all of your transcripts to find patterns here.
Pipegen is a game of timing.

Signals are meant to help you predict who to reach out to at the right time with the right message.

See.. Most GTM leaders are asking the completely wrong question.

It doesn't matter what signals/plays the best performing teams are using.

The absolute BEST signals and plays are unique to your company and market.

Most signals that you see on here are completely commoditized at this point.

Especially when used in isolation…

-Funding
-Headcount growth
-Job Changes
-Generic Intent

The best orgs that I have seen are looking for very specific information that informs their outreach.

1. If you are a Waste Management Company

Looking for orgs that produce X tons of waste in a given year

2. Company that detects Software Outages?

Looking for orgs that have had software outages in a given location within the last 3 months

3. Company that sells Restaurant Software?

Looking for restaurants with low Google ratings and using AI agents to pick out themes of negative reviews

4. Medical Waste and Compliance Training?

Locating high-growth veterinary clinics that have low-compliance ratings and high growth

Most companies are sitting on GOLD within their first party data (CRM, Call Transcripts, Product Usage data)

Take some time to understand WHY your customers bought and go and build these systems.

It's how you truly stand out.

Using assumptive tones in your emails is turning your buyers off and killing your reply rates.

It's something I didn't even realize I was doing and makes sense when thinking it through as a buyer myself.

A lot of the times in emails to prospects we use strong assumptive language like:

-I saw you're hiring. According to {blog}, X% of new sales hires don't hit quota.

-Noticed that while XYZ as an org has been increasing headcount overall, the engineering team has lost a few members in the past year

-It costs about $40k per head to hire an engineer and almost all heads of engineering I speak with are behind on hiring, and it's why retention is critical

The problem with this type of language is that you are a random person in a buyers inbox and the buyer is thinking... "Who the hell is this person, telling me about my business with no context?".

A better approach is to use softer or an "unsure" tone. Here's some examples:

-I saw you might be hiring. I could be wrong, but are you starting to think about ramp?

-I might be off here, but it looks like although your org has been increasing headcount overall, the engineering team has lost a few members in the past year

-This might not be the case for you, but It typically costs about $40k per head to hire an engineer and it's common for most heads of engineering to be behind on hiring

Words like "might", "typically", "it's common" soften the email and gets more replies.

There's a fundamental flaw with how outbound teams approach messaging that I see over and over again.

We've gotten to a place where "signals/triggers" have become a commodity.

The signal should NOT be your message.

The signal is actually just telling you that it COULD be the right time.

Let's say I'm a rep at an observability platform that monitors outages for software teams... I happen to get a pricing page web visit from an ICP persona.

Unfortunately most reps will default to Example 1 which is a massive problem for a few reasons:

1. There is no additional value that a salesperson brings to this interaction that can't be automated by a simple marketing email.

2. Being on your pricing page MIGHT indicate interest, but you can't assume it.

As a salesperson, your job is to take a signal and paint a contextual picture as to why this is the perfect time to evaluate/purchase your solution.

CONTEXT is what is missing in a lot of outbound messaging and sales cycles.

Tell an exec something they don't know or help them connect the dots on something they might be thinking about.

Not enough teams are doing it.
